Sensory depth · breadth-first search

How many synapses from a sense to an action?

Touch reaches the brain’s descending command neurons in about two synapses; taste, smell and vision need about three. Fast mechanosensory routes fit reflexes like grooming and escape.

Shortest paths · strongest bottleneck

From tasting sugar to extending the proboscis

Sugar-sensing taste neurons reach MN9, the motor neuron that extends the fly’s proboscis to feed, in just two synapses. These are the same 21 neurons stimulated in the fly-brain simulation’s sugar experiment.

The brain’s most central neurons

The top of the list belongs to giant, brain-wide neurons. APL and DPM each wire into the entire mushroom body, the fly’s learning centre; CT1 does the same for motion vision in the optic lobe.

Predicted neurotransmitters

What each class of neuron releases

Acetylcholine, the fly’s main excitatory transmitter, dominates nearly everywhere. Visual centrifugal neurons, which send feedback from the central brain into the optic lobes, are mostly GABA-releasing (inhibitory). Endocrine cells are unusually rich in serotonin and octopamine.

Methods and data

Connectivity: FlyWire v783 public release (Dorkenwald et al., Nature 2024), as packaged in eonsystemspbc/fly-brain. Cell types, classes, sides and predicted neurotransmitters: FlyWire annotations (Schlegel et al., Nature 2024). Sugar receptor neurons and MN9 follow Shiu et al. (Nature 2024).

A “connection” is a pair of neurons with at least one synapse; hop counts and paths use only strong connections (≥ 5 synapses). Synapses from GABA- and glutamate-releasing neurons are counted as inhibitory. PageRank is weighted by synapse count (α = 0.85). Symmetry compares left→left with right→right synapses for every pair of cell types found in both hemispheres, keeping pairs with ≥ 50 synapses in total. The soma map draws a 60,000-neuron sample; the smaller classes are shown in full.
